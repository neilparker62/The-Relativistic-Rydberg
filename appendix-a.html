1. Introduction

A central constraint in validating the real-space relativistic trajectories predicted by the Relativistic Rydberg model is the paradox of quantum measurement. The model predicts that electrons in highly structured angular momentum states of singly ionized Helium (\(\text{He II}\)), specifically the \(3d_{5/2}\) and \(4f_{7/2}\) orbitals, possess intrinsic, stable geometric resonance frequencies sitting at \(2.0034 \times 10^{17}\text{ Hz}\) (\(828.53\text{ eV}\)) and \(1.1269 \times 10^{17}\text{ Hz}\) (\(466.05\text{ eV}\)) respectively.

Because these energies vastly exceed the \(54.4\text{ eV}\) ionization threshold of \(\text{He}^+\), any attempt to measure these states using standard on-resonance absorption spectroscopy will transfer a massive energy packet, instantly stripping the electron from the nucleus. On a standard spectrometer, this explosive disruption results in a broad, featureless photoionization continuum, completely masking the sharp, non-decaying geometric line profile. This appendix establishes an advanced, non-destructive optical protocol to measure these sub-atomic cycles while preserving their underlying stability.

2. The Dispersive Non-Destructive Framework

To bypass the photoionization bottleneck, the measurement must shift from the absorptive regime to the dispersive regime. By intentionally detuning the probe laser frequency (\(\nu\)) slightly below the intrinsic resonance frequency (\(\nu_0\)), the system operates in a regime where absorption drops to virtually zero (\(\kappa \to 0\)). The probe photons do not deposit kinetic energy; instead, they undergo coherent forward scattering. The resulting phase shift \(\Delta\Phi\) acts as a continuous measurement of the macroscopic beat frequency (\(\Delta \nu = \nu - \nu_0\)) between the incoming probe radiation field and the internal orbital clock:

$$\Delta n(\Delta \omega) \approx 1 - \frac{N e^2}{2 \epsilon_0 m_e \omega_0} \left[ \frac{\Delta \omega}{(\Delta \omega)^2 + (\gamma/2)^2} \right]$$

Where \(\Delta \omega = 2\pi(\nu - \nu_0)\) is the frequency detuning gap, \(N\) is the target ion density, and \(\gamma\) is the intrinsic linewidth. Because the model dictates that these resonances correspond to stable, non-decaying geometric orbital configurations, \(\gamma\) is predicted to be exceptionally narrow, yielding a steep, localized antisymmetric refractive index swing immediately below the resonance boundary.

3. Attosecond Phase-Dispersion Interferometry

To capture this sub-atomic chronology natively, the setup requires sub-femtosecond resolution. The proposed apparatus utilizes an asymmetric attosecond Mach-Zehnder interferometer layout split across two distinct optical arms:

A co-axial optical pump laser is tuned precisely to the conventional visible 4686Å complex line to ionize the medium to \(\text{He II}\) and selectively populate the \(3d\) and \(4f\) orbital states.
  2. Interrogation (The Probe): High-Harmonic Generation (HHG) driven by a femtosecond seed laser produces broadband, coherent soft X-ray pulses with a duration of \(50 - 100\text{ attoseconds}\). This probe beam is split into a reference arm traveling through vacuum and a probe arm focused directly through the excited \(\text{He II}\) gas jet.

The probe photon profile is configured to target the optimal detuning windows (\(\delta E \approx -0.5\text{ eV}\) below resonance), placing the probe beam at \(465.55\text{ eV}\) and \(828.03\text{ eV}\) respectively. The interaction length \(L\) through the jet imparts a spatial phase delay onto the probe wavefront. When the probe and reference arms recombine at a grazing-incidence beam splitter, the real part of the index shift is read out as a sharp spatial fringe displacement on an X-ray CCD camera array:

$$\Delta \Phi(\omega) = \frac{\omega L}{c} \Delta n(\omega)$$

Because the gas jet contains a high background density of direct photoionization events, a static phase measurement would be prone to systematic drift and instrumental line blurring. $$\Delta f_{\text{Zeeman}} = \frac{2 \mu_B B}{h} \approx 28.0\text{ GHz per Tesla}$$

While the broad, featureless background of standard photoionization is completely uncoupled from the magnetic field, the steep dispersion slopes of the \(3d_{5/2}\) and \(4f_{7/2}\) resonances will modulate aggressively at \(10\text{ kHz}\). By routing the output of the X-ray CCD pixels through a differential lock-in amplifier loop tuned to \(10\text{ kHz}\), the common-mode plasma background is suppressed, forcing the sharp, stable geometric resonance peaks to stand out with pristine clarity.
