1. Symmetry and Convergence with the Dirac Equation

The relativistic energy eigenvalue spectrum for a single-electron atom derived from the Dirac equation is expressed as:

$$E_{n,j} = \mu c^2 \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{Z\alpha}{n - |k| + \sqrt{k^2 - Z^2\alpha^2}} \right)^2 \right]^{-1/2}$$

Where \(n\) is the principal quantum number and \(k\) is the relativistic angular momentum integer. Within the Relativistic Rydberg framework, applying a classical-relativistic quadrature relation between orbital kinetic energy and transverse radiant wave energy yields the relation:

$$E_n = \mu c^2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{n^2}}$$

Under this constraint, the term \(n - |k|\) vanishes to zero, and Equation 1 reduces algebraically to Equation 2. This demonstrates formal mathematical alignment between the geometric derivation and the Dirac eigenvalue spectrum under maximum angular momentum configurations.

3. Mathematical Integrity of the Derivation

The mathematical structure relies on standard physical constants: the elementary charge (\(e\)), electron rest mass (\(m_0\)), speed of light (\(c\)), and fine-structure constant (\(\alpha\)).

The velocity wave equation \(v(t) = c\sin(\frac{Ft}{m_0 c})\) integrates to spatial displacement \(x(t)\). A binomial expansion of the core energy relation yields:

$$\mu c^2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{n^2}} \approx \mu c^2 - \frac{\mu c^2 Z^2 \alpha^2}{2 n^2}$$

This expansion recovers the classical non-relativistic Rydberg energy expression as a limiting case, demonstrating structural consistency with established low-energy formulas.
