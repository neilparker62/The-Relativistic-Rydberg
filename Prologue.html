2. The Conventional Relativistic Formulation of Newton II

In standard textbook physics, when a particle with rest mass $m_0$ moves at a relativistic velocity $\vec{v}$, its linear momentum is defined as $\vec{p} = \gamma m_0 \vec{v}$. When applying Newton’s Second Law, standard physics defines force as the total time derivative of this momentum vector:

$$F = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{m_{0}\vec{v}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}}\right)$$

Assuming one-dimensional linear motion where the net force $\vec{F}$ acts strictly parallel to the velocity vector $\vec{v}$, executing the calculus chain rule yields:

$$F = \frac{m_{0}}{\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)^{3/2}}\frac{dv}{dt} = \gamma^{3} m_{0}\frac{dv}{dt}$$

Feeding this standard differential equation to a mathematical engine under a constant, sustained linear force $F$ yields a hyperbolic trajectory:

$$v(t) = \frac{Ft}{\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}+\frac{F^{2}t^{2}}{c^{2}}}}$$

As $t \to \infty$, $v(t)$ asymptotically approaches the speed of light $c$ without ever exceeding it. For an un-bound, free particle traveling linearly down a particle accelerator, this formulation is entirely accurate.

3. The Pure Lorentz Mass Variant Formulation

The alternative model poses a fundamentally distinct mechanical question by evaluating the second common representation of Newton's Second Law, $F = m \cdot a$. $$F = m_{\text{rel}} \cdot a = \left(\frac{m_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v(t)^{2}}{c^{2}}}}\right)\frac{dv(t)}{dt}$$

When this specific differential arrangement is evaluated, the mathematical solution shatters the asymptotic velocity barrier, emerging as a purely periodic wave equation:

$$v(t) = c\sin \left(\frac{Ft}{m_{0}c}\right)$$

5. Derivation of the Bohr Magneton and Zeeman Splitting

To map this geometric wave motion onto observable macroscopic quantum mechanics, we calculate the magnetic dipole moment ($\mu_m$) generated by this circulating charge. For a localized current loop carrying a charge $-e$ traversing a closed path of radius $r$ at velocity $v$, the effective electrical current $I$ and loop area $A$ are:

$$I = \frac{-e}{T} = \frac{-ev}{2\pi r}, \quad A = \pi r^2$$

The resulting magnetic dipole moment is the product of current and area:

$$\mu_m = I \cdot A = \left(\frac{-ev}{2\pi r}\right)\left(\pi r^2\right) = -\frac{1}{2}evr$$

Substituting the orbital quantum parameters—where the path is bounded by the reduced Compton radius ($r = \bar{\lambda}_e = \frac{\hbar}{m_e c}$) and the tangential velocity scales as $v = \sqrt{\alpha}c$—yields:

$$\mu_m = -\frac{1}{2}e\left(\sqrt{\alpha}c\right)\left(\frac{\hbar}{m_e c}\right) = -\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\frac{e\hbar}{2m_e}\right)$$

The expression within the parentheses defines the fundamental constant of atomic magnetism, the Bohr magneton ($\mu_B$):

$$\mu_B = \frac{e\hbar}{2m_e} \approx 5.7884 \times 10^{-5}\text{ eV/T}$$

By integrating the multi-turn solenoidal winding correction ($N = \sqrt{137} = 1/\sqrt{\alpha}$) required by a continuous 3D spiral trajectory, the total integrated magnetic dipole moment reconciles perfectly with macroscopic quantum measurements:

$$\mu_{\text{total}} = N \times \mu_m = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\left(-\sqrt{\alpha}\mu_B\right) = -\mu_B$$

When an external magnetic field ($B$) is applied to this bound system, it couples to the total magnetic moment via the interaction Hamiltonian $\Delta E = -\vec{\mu}_{\text{total}} \cdot \vec{B}$. The total energy splitting between the outer polarized components ($\sigma^+$ and $\sigma^-$) of an optical transition line evaluates to:

$$\Delta E_{\text{Zeeman}} = 2\mu_B B$$

Dividing by Planck's constant ($h$) yields the absolute, invariant frequency splitting per unit field strength:

$$\Delta f_{\text{Zeeman}} = \frac{2\mu_B}{h} \approx \mathbf{28.0\text{ GHz per Tesla}}$$

Crucially, this result is entirely independent of the underlying atomic transition frequency. This constant $28.0\text{ GHz/Tesla}$ shift is universally verified across high-field Tokamak diagnostics, providing a definitive spatial signature to verify the existence of the core orbital geometry.
