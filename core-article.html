Abstract

This monograph presents an independent heuristic method for the derivation of the "relativistic Rydberg" formula, applicable to Hydrogen and hydrogen-like atoms. Operating from a modified representation of Newton's Second Law where the Lorentz factor resides within the scalar mass coefficient ($F = m_{rel} \cdot a$), the system resolves natively into an electromagnetic velocity wave equation. We demonstrate that under the maximum energy constraints of a bound primary quantum shell ($l_{max} = n-1, j_{max} = l+1/2$), the complex multi-component spinor Dirac equation collapses algebraically into this streamlined scalar formulation: $E_n = \mu c^2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{Z^2 \alpha^2}{n^2}}$. This approach unifies real-space orbits with observed quantum transitions to an accuracy of nine significant figures without ad-hoc parameter tuning.

1. Introduction

In a number of previous articles, this author has made use of an equation for various atomic transition frequencies/energies pertaining to Hydrogen or 'hydrogen-like' atoms such as singly ionized Helium. It was shown that this equation is an off-shoot of the more general Dirac energy equation corresponding to the maximum possible energy of an electron in any particular energy level for which the primary quantum number is $n$.

The equation does not depend on any of the other quantum numbers since these simply cancel out under the maximum energy constraint. Since the older Rydberg equation also does not take into account anything other than the primary quantum number, it seems appropriate to name this version the "relativistic Rydberg". It will simply prove more accurate since it does take into account the relativistic nature of an electron bound to an atomic nucleus. There is in fact a direct mathematical relationship between the two: the traditional Rydberg equation emerges from a one-term binomial expansion of the "relativistic Rydberg".

2. Relativistic Velocity Formulations under Newton II

Standard textbook physics defines force as the total time derivative of the linear relativistic momentum vector ($F = dp/dt$), leading to asymptotic, hyperbolic free-particle trajectories. In this paper, we explore the physical implications of including the velocity-dependent Lorentz factor directly inside the mass coefficient of the Newtonian expression:

$$F = \frac{m_{0}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v(t)^{2}}{c^{2}}}}\frac{dv(t)}{dt}$$

Solving this differential relation under massive, perpendicular electrostatic Coulomb force fields yields a straightforward wave equation for particle velocity [5]:

$$v(t) = c \sin\left(\frac{Ft + k_1 m_0}{m_0 c}\right)$$

A Taylor series expansion of this expression reveals that classical Newtonian mechanics emerges cleanly as a low-energy limiting case ($v(t) \approx \frac{Ft}{m_0} = at$), while the high-frequency term highlights that wave-like behavior becomes manifest exclusively under intense sub-atomic force-to-mass fields.

3. The Mass-Energy Quadrature

Integrating our velocity wave expression translates the cyclical coordinate runtime across the orbit into a bounded energy equation. By multiplying through by the inward electrostatic pulling force ($F$), we establish that the electron's classical kinetic vis viva energy ($m_e v^2$) stands in structural quadrature with its internal wave component ($hf$), bounding the system parameters along a right-angled Mass-Energy Triangle:

$$(m_e v^2)^2 + (hf)^2 = (m_e c^2)^2$$

Rearranging this Pythagorean relationship to isolate the radiant wave energy component yields:

$$hf = \sqrt{(m_e c^2)^2 - (m_e v^2)^2}$$

Substituting the parameterized variables derived from an electron constrained at the reduced Compton radius ($v^2 = \alpha c^2$), the baseline ground state wave energy scales as $hf = m_e c^2 \sqrt{1 - \alpha^2}$ [2].

4. Quantization and Spectral Convergence

Extending this framework across higher energy states requires that the spatial dimensions scale according to the primary quantum boundary condition ($r_n = n\overline{\lambda}_e$). For an arbitrary shell $n$, the potential energy splits fluidly, expanding our relationship into the general Relativistic Rydberg formula:

$$E_n = \mu c^2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{Z^2 \alpha^2}{n^2}}$$

A single-term binomial expansion of this square root resolves perfectly into the historical, non-relativistic expression used in conventional chemistry ($\mu c^2 - \frac{\mu c^2 Z^2 \alpha^2}{2n^2}$), proving absolute continuity with established physical principles.
