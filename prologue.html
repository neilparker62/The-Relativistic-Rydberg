2. The Conventional Relativistic Formulation vs. Pure Lorentz Mass

In standard textbook physics, when a particle with rest mass \(m_0\) moves at a relativistic velocity \(\vec{v}\), its linear momentum is defined as \(\vec{p} = \gamma m_0 \vec{v}\). When applying Newton's Second Law, standard physics defines force as the total time derivative of this momentum vector:

$$F = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{m_0 \vec{v}}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} \right)$$

As demonstrated in this framework, taking the full vector derivative yields asymptotic, hyperbolic trajectories rather than stable bound orbits. By isolating the pure Lorentz mass variant where \(F = m_{\text{rel}} \cdot a\) with \(m_{\text{rel}} = \gamma m_0\), the intense perpendicular geometric constraints of a Coulomb field force the momentum derivative into a stable periodic velocity wave equation.
